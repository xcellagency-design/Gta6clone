# UNDERTOW: Port Meridian — Story Bible

Working title: *Project Vice*. This is the narrative reference for the whole team. It describes the
fiction; the machine-readable truth lives in `data/narrative/decision_graph.json`,
`data/narrative/ending_registry.json`, `data/narrative/characters.json` and
`data/missions/side_missions.json`. Where this document and the data disagree, the data wins and
this document has a bug. The twenty endings are specified in full in `docs/ENDING_DESIGN.md`.

UNDERTOW is an original setting. Every person, company, agency, place and event below is invented.

---

## 1. Logline and pillars

**Logline.** Three years after a hurricane drowned half of Port Meridian, a tow-truck driver comes
home to find the family salvage yard seized with a forged signature from their dead mother, and
has to decide what they are willing to become to get it back.

**The question the game asks.** Not "can Cass get the yard back?" (several factions can hand it over
with one phone call) but "what will the name Navarro mean once they do?"

**Pillars.**

1. **Salvage, not conquest.** Cass's verbs are towing, hauling, raising and recovering. Even the
   crimes are repossessions of a sort. The fantasy is competence with heavy things, not firepower.
2. **Everybody has a reason.** No faction is simply evil. The smugglers feed the port, the developer
   really is rebuilding the city, the corrupt cops really are underpaid, the organizers really can be
   insufferable. The player should be tempted by all four.
3. **The bill always arrives.** Every favor in Port Meridian is a loan. Choices pay out acts later,
   usually when a storm is coming.
4. **Small coast, long memory.** Twelve named characters who all know each other. Nobody is a
   stranger, which is what makes betrayal expensive.

## 2. The setting

### 2.1 Port Meridian

A fictional Gulf-coast American metropolis of about 900,000 people, built where a slow brown river
meets a shallow bay. It is hot ten months of the year and beautiful for about forty minutes each
evening. The money has always come from three places: the port (Ironhook, the fourth-busiest
container terminal on the Gulf), the beach (Sol Strand and the hotels behind it) and, since the
storm, the federal government.

The city sits low. Meridian Center and Crown Shore are on the only real high ground. The Flats is
called the Flats because it is: four square miles of shotgun houses and corner stores, eighteen
inches above mean high tide. On a king tide the water comes up through the storm drains under a
cloudless sky, and people move their cars without looking up from their phones.

Two pieces of highway hold the place together: the **I-9 Loop**, which rings the urban districts,
and the **Odile Causeway**, the rebuilt six-mile bridge to Saltgrass County that was renamed, with
the city's usual sense of humor, after the storm that destroyed the old one.

### 2.2 Hurricane Odile

Three years before the game begins, Hurricane Odile came ashore at Port Meridian as a Category 4
storm at high tide. The surge went through Ironhook Port and across the Flats in under an hour.
The Eastbank levee, which had been "scheduled for assessment" for eleven years, failed in two
places. Two hundred and nineteen people died. Three hundred of the living spent the night in a
church hall on Mercy Street that later became the Flats Collective.

In the weeks afterward the harbor was unusable: sunken shrimp boats, containers, a casino barge
and nine of the Tideline's own vessels blocked the channel. The outfit that cleared it, mostly on
credit and mostly without contracts, was **Navarro Salvage** of Ironhook Pier 9, run by **Inés
Navarro** with her foreman Salvatore Dupree and her eldest child on the winch. Inés raised a
trawler belonging to Odalys Ferrer's nephew and never collected. She died of a heart attack the
following November, on the dock, with a clipboard in her hand.

### 2.3 The Rebuild

Congress appropriated eleven billion dollars. The state created the **Meridian Rebuild Authority**
to spend it, and the Authority awarded the master contract for the **Eastbank Rebuild Zone** to
**Crestline Development Group**. Everyone in the city calls the money, the agency and the
construction, collectively, "the Rebuild."

The Rebuild's engine is a single ordinance. The **Debris Recovery Ordinance**, drafted in good faith
by Crestline's general counsel Vivian Cho in the month after the storm, lets the Authority clear
hazardous storm debris from private land and bill the owner, securing the bill with a
**remediation lien**. An owner who cannot pay within a year can be foreclosed on, and the Authority
may assign the property to its master developer.

Graham Whitlock discovered what the ordinance could be used for. The scheme works like this:

1. Crestline identifies a parcel it wants: Eastbank row houses, the Flats frontage, a pier.
2. A contractor (usually Halvorsen Site Services, a Crestline subsidiary) files a remediation
   invoice for debris removal that never happened, or that somebody else did.
3. The owner's consent form is produced, notarized by **Dale Pruitt**, a notary on Crestline's
   retainer who stamped some two hundred blank forms in one afternoon.
4. The lien is recorded. A year later the property goes to auction and Crestline is the only
   bidder who knew it was coming.
5. Captain Royce Halloran's Harbor Unit serves the notices and keeps the peace at the evictions.
   The Patrolmen's Benevolent Association "building fund" receives a payment on the first of every
   month.

There are **206** such liens. Journalist Priya Raman collects them. Her favorite is number 141:
Navarro Salvage, Pier 9, billed $412,000 for the removal of marine debris that Navarro Salvage
itself removed, on a consent form signed by Inés Navarro in March, four months after her funeral.

### 2.4 Hurricane Pilar

The game takes place across one hurricane season. From the start of Act 2 the car radio carries
tropical outlooks; by the end of Act 2 a system has a name. Act 3 is thirty-six hours of a city
preparing badly for **Hurricane Pilar**, and the finale happens in and after landfall. The storm
is not a punishment and not a metaphor anyone in the game is allowed to say out loud. It is cover:
every faction uses the evacuation to do the thing it could not do in daylight.

## 3. Themes and tone

**Themes.**

- **Recovery as extraction.** Who gets rebuilt, who gets "remediated" and who signs the invoices.
- **Inheritance.** A yard, a debt, a name, a neighborhood, a criminal organization, an empty chair:
  nearly every ending is about what Cass inherits or refuses to.
- **What a thing is worth to the person who lost it.** The salvage operator's whole trade, and the
  fixer's, and the developer's.
- **Loyalty versus use.** Every patron offers belonging and means employment. Sal and Jun offer
  neither and are the only ones who would burn for Cass.

**Tone.** A grounded crime drama with weather. Mature without being grim for its own sake: people
are funny under pressure, and the humor is dry, local and usually at the expense of paperwork.
Violence is rare, quick and has consequences that last. Nobody monologues. Nobody is a psychopath.
The most frightening people in the game are polite.

**Voice rules for writers.**

- Dialogue lines are short and are written as `Speaker: text`. Two to five lines per exchange.
- Cass is dry and practical and never explains their feelings. Cass's gender is never stated; in
  prose use "they" or restructure the sentence. Other characters say "Cass", "Navarro", "kid"
  (Sal) or "Inés's child" (Mama Tide).
- Every character gets one verbal habit and keeps it: Brandt issues instructions ("Do not use the
  Causeway"), Whitlock compliments before he threatens, Mama Tide feeds people before she
  threatens, Vivian describes crimes in the passive voice, Nico calls everyone "my friend", Sal
  insults as affection, Toby runs everything as an agenda item.
- No real people, brands, gangs, agencies beyond the generic (Coast Guard, U.S. Marshals, DMV),
  and no slurs. Invented brands recur: Gulfline (auto shop and finance company), Halvorsen Site
  Services, the Meridian Courier, the Carrusel, Alder & Brine, the Lantern Room.

## 4. Factions

Each faction can return the yard. Each one charges differently.

### 4.1 The Tideline (`tideline`)

**What it is.** The port's smuggling syndicate: about two hundred dock workers, boat crews and
captains who move untaxed goods, diverted relief supplies and, lately, pharmaceuticals through
Ironhook. Older than the container terminal. Organized like a fishing fleet: six captains, one
dock boss and **Odalys "Mama Tide" Ferrer** at the corner table of Ferrer's Fish House on Pier 2.

**What it wants.** For the port to stay a port. Crestline's harborfront phase would replace working
piers with a marina village, and the Tideline with security guards.

**How it works.** Favors, food and exact accounting. It pays the Harbor Unit a weekly "inspection
fee" and resents the recent increase. Membership is by oath, sworn kneeling on the fish-house
floor. The oath has no exit clause; this is not a figure of speech.

**What it offers Cass.** One phone call from Mama Tide and the lien is "withdrawn in error."
**What it costs.** The yard becomes a front and Cass becomes hers.

### 4.2 Crestline Development Group (`crestline`)

**What it is.** A developer consortium headquartered on the top floors of Crestline Tower in
Meridian Center. Master developer of the Eastbank Rebuild Zone. Chief executive **Graham
Whitlock**; general counsel **Vivian Cho**.

**What it wants.** Contiguous waterfront from Eastbank through the Flats frontage to Ironhook: the
land its financing model assumes it already has. Pier 9 and Mercy Street are the two holes in the
map.

**How it works.** Law used as a construction material. Liens, condemnation orders, emergency
powers, settlements with releases attached. It never breaks a window. It has Halloran for that.

**What it offers Cass.** "Friendly review" of the lien, then a salary, then a title.
**What it costs.** Being the person who does to other families what was done to the Navarros.

### 4.3 Meridian PD (`mpd`)

A department with two centers of gravity.

- **Captain Royce Halloran** commands the Harbor Unit and runs the Patrolmen's Benevolent
  Association from its hall in Meridian Center. He collects from the Tideline on Thursdays and from
  Crestline on the first of the month, and regards both as a pension plan the city forgot to fund.
  `rep_mpd` measures Cass's standing with *this* side.
- **Detective Lena Brandt** works Financial Crimes with no budget and no approval, building a
  fraud and racketeering case out of lien filings. She meets sources on level four of the
  courthouse garage because Halloran's clerk reads the informant registry. `trust_brandt` measures
  Cass's standing with her.

**What Brandt offers.** A lien vacated by a judge instead of by a favor.
**What it costs.** Being known, forever, as the one who wore the wire.
**What Halloran offers.** A paycheck, a placard and protection. **What it costs.** Brandt.

### 4.4 The Flats Collective (`flats`)

**What it is.** A mutual-aid organization that grew out of the Mercy Street shelter on the night of
Odile. Food pantry, clinic volunteer schedule, tool library, tenant defense and a Thursday meeting
that has never once ended on time. Led, to the extent that it is led, by **Tobias "Toby" Akana**.

**What it wants.** For the Flats to still be there, and to belong to the people in it. Its endgame
is the **Mercy Street Community Land Trust** buying its own block (Lot Fourteen) at the Rebuild
Authority's lien auction.

**How it works.** Numbers, cameras and stubbornness. It is not a gang and Toby will not let it
become one, though it has members who would not mind.

**What it offers Cass.** A folding chair, and people who will stand at a gate at six in the morning.
**What it costs.** Private ownership. In the Collective's best ending the yard is saved and no
Navarro will ever inherit it.

### 4.5 Unaligned

**Nico Valdés** is a faction of one: a broker of introductions who has never taken a side. **Priya
Raman** and the Meridian Courier are the only institution in the city that nobody is paying.

## 5. Characters

Ids in `code font` are the ids used in every data file.

### `cass` — Cass Navarro (protagonist)

Mid-thirties. Grew up two doors from Toby on Mercy Street, learned to dive at fourteen and to weld
at fifteen, left the yard after an argument with Inés and spent a decade driving repo and tow for
Gulfline Finance. Came back when she died. Rents a room over the Mercy Street laundromat.

- **Wants:** the yard back. **Needs:** to decide what the yard was for.
- **Voice:** dry, exact, allergic to speeches. "I tow cars for a living."
- **Arc:** written by the player. The tracked variables are, in effect, Cass's character sheet:
  who they are loyal to, who is afraid of them and how much of it can be proved in court.
- **Fates:** kingpin, legitimate owner, protected witness, convict, corpse, exile, fixer, depot
  manager, drifter, lieutenant, police contractor, vindicated felon, fugitive, club owner, small
  businessperson, organizer, paid-off nobody, keeper of a truce, debtor to an old man, repo driver.

### `jun` — Jun Navarro

Twenty-six. Mechanic at Gulfline Auto in Cypress Heights; builds engines for the Neon Mile sprint
crowd at night. Was seventeen when Cass left and has never entirely forgiven it.

- **Wants:** to be asked to help. **Fear:** being cargo.
- **Arc:** `trust_jun` tracks whether Cass treats him as a partner. High trust makes him capable:
  he gets himself out of Eastbank alone and will follow Cass onto the last boat. Low trust makes
  him act alone, which is how he ends up bleeding in a site office in "Hour Zero."
- **Can die:** only in "Hour Zero," and only if Cass chooses to wait. His death unlocks "The Wake."

### `sal` — Salvatore "Old Sal" Dupree

Seventy-one. Navarro Salvage's foreman for thirty-four years, a retired commercial diver, in love
with Inés for most of that time without ever saying it. Lives on a shrimp boat at the foot of
Pier 9 and sits at the chained gate every morning with a thermos.

- **Wants:** to keep the gate. **Need:** to know Inés's eldest is worth the trouble.
- **Arc:** `loyalty_sal` starts at 1 (he is still deciding) and rises when Cass protects the yard
  or the family over profit. At 3 he will do anything, including walk into police headquarters in
  his funeral suit and confess to every crime Cass committed ("The Old Man's Debt").
- **Can die:** in the yard fire in "Storm Warning," if Cass goes to Jun instead.

### `toby` — Tobias "Toby" Akana

Thirty-six. Cass's oldest friend. Was on a roof on Mercy Street with Cass the night of Odile and
decided, up there, never to be quiet again. Has not taken an evening off since.

- **Wants:** Lot Fourteen. **Flaw:** believes everyone can be organized, including Cass.
- **Arc:** `trust_toby` tracks whether he still picks up the phone. He is the conscience Cass keeps
  trying to outsource; routes that betray the Flats put him physically in the way.
- **Can die:** under the Collective Hall if Cass orders the demolition ("Emergency Powers"), or in
  the cut if he makes the last rescue run himself ("High Ground").

### `brandt` — Detective Lena Brandt

Forty-two. Financial Crimes. Came up through patrol under Halloran and knows precisely how the
envelope works because she was once offered a share. Precise, tired, speaks in instructions.

- **Wants:** one count in front of one jury. **Cost:** she will use Cass the way everyone else does,
  only with forms.
- **Fates:** vindicated and promoted (wire routes), dismissed and exiled to insurance work ("Blue
  Wall"), or simply left adding names to the chart on her wall.

### `halloran` — Captain Royce Halloran

Fifty-seven. Commander of the Harbor Unit, treasurer and later president of the PBA. Lives in
Crown Shore on a captain's salary. Genial, patient and wholly transactional. "I knew your mother."

- **Wants:** stability, defined as everyone paying on time.
- **Rule:** he only employs people he has already paid. This is literal: "Blue Wall" requires
  `rep_mpd` of at least 1 before Cass gives him Brandt. Otherwise he takes the gift and drops the
  giver.
- **Fates:** arrested on Cass's wire, retired under a cloud after the Odile Papers, or untouchable.

### `mama_tide` — Odalys "Mama Tide" Ferrer

Sixty-eight. Has run the Tideline from a corner table for thirty years. Feeds people before she
threatens them and keeps exact accounts of both. Calls Cass "Inés's child" until they earn a name.

- **Wants:** a successor she does not have to be afraid of. Has begun to suspect she cannot have both.
- **Fates:** undisputed with Cass at her right hand; dead by Cass's order (succeeded or avenged by
  her captains depending on whether Cass has the standing to hold the chair); or simply patient.

### `whitlock` — Graham Whitlock

Fifty-four. Chief executive of Crestline. Sincerely believes he is saving the city and that the
people in the way will thank him. Names his yacht *Second Act*. Compliments your appeal before
explaining why it will fail.

- **Wants:** the map filled in, then the mayor's office.
- **Fates:** mayoral candidate, stalled tycoon, indicted, convicted or thrown through his own
  window wall into a hurricane.

### `priya` — Priya Raman

Thirty-one. Reporter for the Meridian Courier. Collects forged liens. Honest about the fact that
the story matters to her more than most of the people in it, and likable anyway.

- **Wants:** the green ledger, or a source willing to be named.
- **Fates:** publishes the Odile Papers; kills the only story she has ever killed, at Sal's request;
  or keeps the folder open with nobody on the record.

### `nico` — Nico Valdés

Forty. Broker, minority owner of the Lantern Room, friend of everyone. Fronts Cass's appeal bond
in the first hour and is still, in most endings, owed a favor he never collects. Leaves town on
the last boat the night of Pilar in every timeline; whether he comes back depends on who is running
the port.

- **Wants:** to know who is holding what. "That's the whole business, Cass."

### `deck` — Isaiah "Deck" Marlowe

Fifty-eight. Tideline dock boss. Thirty years carrying Mama Tide's water; has taken one meeting
with Crestline without asking permission, which in the Tideline is a capital offense. Dislikes
Cass as "the new favorite."

- **Fates:** killed by Cass on the dredge-spoil islands, put on a bus, or delivered to Brandt's
  garage as a witness ("Blood in the Bilge").

### `vivian` — Vivian Cho

Forty-six. Crestline's general counsel. Wrote an honest statute, then billed by the hour while it
became a foreclosure machine. Keeps the **Odile Ledger**, a green bound book reconciling every
forged lien and every payoff, in the galley safe of Whitlock's yacht: insurance or penance, she has
not decided which.

- **Wants:** an exit that does not end with her as the only defendant.
- **Fates:** disbarred cooperating witness, interim chief executive, or still general counsel with
  a new file open on Cass.

### Minor and off-screen

- **Inés Navarro** (deceased): the standard everyone measures Cass against. Her salvage logbooks
  are a recurring object; they hold the bearings of the old Navarro moorings off Sol Strand.
- **Dale Pruitt:** the notary. Timid, fond of his cat, stamped two hundred blank forms over lunch.
- **The Mayor:** never named, never seen; signs what Crestline drafts.
- **Halvorsen Site Services:** Crestline's contractor subsidiary. **Gulfline Finance:** Cass's old
  repo employer and the fallback ending's new one.

## 7. Structure

Three acts and a finale: 33 nodes in the decision graph, of which one playthrough visits 16 to 18
(15 to 17 decisions). Act 1 is shared. Act 2 and Act 3 each split four ways by patron, with a
defection available at the end of each Act 2 branch, so a player can finish with a different
faction from the one they started with. Shared missions sit between the branches so that every
route meets the ledger, Jun and the storm.

```
Act 1 (6 shared) ─▶ Act 2 branch (2) ─▶ The Odile Ledger ─▶ Act 2 branch (1) ─▶ Night Shift
      ─▶ Storm Warning (─▶ Hour Zero) ─▶ Act 3 endgame (2) (─▶ The Wake) ─▶ Landfall ─▶ ending
```

### 7.1 Act 1 — High Water Mark

1. **Notice to Vacate** (`a1_notice`, story, Sal). Cass hauls a repossessed boat home and finds the
   gate chained. *Choices:* cut the chain tonight (heat, Sal's loyalty); file the fraud complaint
   downtown (Brandt takes the statement); call Toby and make it the neighborhood's problem.
2. **A Friend of Everyone** (`a1_nico`, decision). Nico fronts the $5,000 appeal bond. *Choices:*
   ask for the dock introduction; ask for Crestline courier work; take the bond and nothing else
   (Sal approves).
3. **Dead Freight** (`a1_freight`, delivery, Nico). A reefer truck over the Causeway. The cargo is
   Rebuild relief stock that never reached the Flats. *Choices:* deliver it; drop two pallets at
   the Mercy Street clinic first; photograph every manifest for the fraud tip line, then deliver.
4. **Torque** (`a1_torque`, character, Jun). A sprint down the Neon Mile in Jun's home-built coupe.
   *Choices:* bring Jun in as mechanic; keep him out of it; put the winnings into a garage lease
   in his name.
5. **Groundbreaking** (`a1_ground`, investigation, Priya). Tow the notary's sedan out of Crestline's
   VIP lot and take his stamp book. *Choices:* give it to Priya; walk it into Brandt's office; let
   Nico sell it back to Crestline.
6. **Three Invitations** (`a1_doors`, decision). *Choices:* sit at Mama Tide's table (Tideline
   branch); get into Whitlock's car (Crestline branch); turn them both down. Refusing leads to the
   Brandt branch if `trust_brandt` is 2 or more (two of: the complaint, the manifests, the stamp
   book) and otherwise to the Flats branch. Choosing a patron burns bridges: the other side's
   standing resets to zero and Brandt throws the folder away.

### 7.2 Act 2 — Undertow

**Tideline branch**

- **Fish House Rules** (`a2_tide_1`, delivery). A night boat run for four sealed totes with Deck
  counting. *Choices:* deliver all four; let one go over the side for the legal fund.
- **The Weigh-In** (`a2_tide_2`, pursuit). Carry the old envelope to Halloran, who wants double.
  *Choices:* cover the difference yourself; bring it back and tell her he is squeezing; record him
  for Brandt (the only way to unlock the defection below).
- **Blood in the Bilge** (`a2_tide_3`, story). Take Deck to the spoil islands and come back alone.
  *Choices:* do it and take the oath; put Deck on a bus, lie and take the oath; take Deck to Brandt
  (defect to the Brandt endgame as an informant, marked by the Tideline).

**Crestline branch**

- **Right of Way** (`a2_crest_1`, business). Tow forty-one holdout vehicles out of Eastbank.
  *Choices:* all of them by Friday; only the abandoned ones.
- **Counsel** (`a2_crest_2`, character, Vivian). A one-a.m. drive to a storage unit. *Choices:*
  tell Whitlock where she keeps her files; keep her secret (she makes some camera footage vanish).
- **Clearance** (`a2_crest_3`, story). Serve a condemnation order on the Collective Hall, with
  Toby on the steps. *Choices:* serve it; find a defect and buy him a week; park the flatbed across
  the street and stand with him (defect to the Flats endgame).

**Brandt branch**

- **Confidential** (`a2_badge_1`, investigation). Photograph the Harbor Unit's Thursday pickups.
  *Choices:* by the book; or also lift the bag. Either way Cass becomes her informant.
- **Blue Flu** (`a2_badge_2`, pursuit). Drive the notary and his cat to a Saltgrass motel ahead of
  the Harbor Unit. *Choices:* sit up all night on watch; take Halloran's call, sell him the wrong
  motel and keep the line open (first Halloran money).
- **Body Wire** (`a2_badge_3`, investigation). Vivian offers $250,000 for a signed release across a
  steakhouse table while Brandt listens. *Choices:* refuse; sign and take the money (defect to the
  Crestline endgame); play the tape for Halloran first (double agent).

**Flats branch**

- **Thursday Meeting** (`a2_flats_1`, delivery). Take back sixty stolen generators. *Choices:*
  quietly at shift change; at noon, on camera, with the block watching.
- **Mercy Street** (`a2_flats_2`, pursuit). Tow a Tideline dealer's car to Mama Tide's own door.
  *Choices:* trade silence for the corner; burn the car on her pier; give the stash to Brandt.
- **The Count** (`a2_flats_3`, business). The land trust is $180,000 short; Crestline's payroll run
  carries exactly that. *Choices:* give it all to the trust; keep half for the lien fight; leave
  the bags and borrow from Mama Tide instead (defect to the Tideline endgame, un-sworn).

**Shared**

- **The Odile Ledger** (`a2_ledger`, investigation, Nico), played between the second and third
  branch missions. Take Vivian's green ledger from the galley safe of *Second Act*. *Choices:* keep
  it and tell no one (the game's most important inventory item); trade it to Nico for his 40%
  stake in the Lantern Room; take only the Navarro page and put the rest back.
- **Night Shift** (`a2_jun`, pursuit, Jun), which closes the act. Pull Jun and a councilman's
  stolen coupe out of a Harbor Unit trap. *Choices:* make him a full partner; send him to Saltgrass;
  put the car on your own record.

### 7.3 Act 3 — Landfall

- **Storm Warning** (`a3_storm`, story, Sal). The yard office is firebombed with Sal inside while
  Jun is cornered in Eastbank. *Choices:* go through the fire for Sal; leave the yard to burn and
  get to Jun (Sal dies); call Toby and split the job (needs `trust_toby` 2+; everyone lives, the
  Flats pays). If Cass saves Sal and Jun's trust is then 1 or less, Jun did not get himself out
  and the story detours to "Hour Zero."
- **Hour Zero** (`a3_eastbank`, pursuit, Jun; conditional). *Choices:* ram the fence (heat +2);
  trade the ledger for him (if held); wait for the guards to rotate (Jun dies).

**Tideline endgame**

- **Dead Reckoning** (`a3_tide_1`, delivery). Hide the Tideline's whole inventory on the old Navarro
  moorings. *Choices:* moor everything where she said; moor one container where only you can find it.
- **The Fish House** (`a3_tide_2`, decision). *Choices:* stand with Mama Tide (the yard, the oath);
  take the chair (she dies; the port follows you only with notoriety 3 and Tideline rep 1+); sell
  the moorings to Whitlock (fatal if you ever took the oath and then stay in town).

**Crestline endgame**

- **Emergency Powers** (`a3_crest_1`, driving). Lead the demolition convoy to Mercy Street.
  *Choices:* give the order (Toby dies in the stairwell); jam the lowboy and blame the weather.
- **The Thirty-First Floor** (`a3_crest_2`, decision). *Choices:* sign and take the title; back
  Vivian and burn him from inside (fraud exposed, yard returned, your own record exposed with it);
  tear up the contract and walk out with what you can carry.

**Brandt endgame**

- **Chain of Custody** (`a3_badge_1`, pursuit). Get eleven evidence boxes over the Causeway before
  it closes. *Choices:* deliver all eleven; lose box seven, the one with your own jobs in it.
- **Wire** (`a3_badge_2`, decision). *Choices:* wear the wire into the union hall (Halloran
  arrested, fraud proven, yard returned); give Priya the ledger (if held); take Halloran's offer
  and give him Brandt.

**Flats endgame**

- **High Ground** (`a3_flats_1`, driving). The Collective evacuates itself. Forty people are left
  across a flooding cut. *Choices:* make the run yourself; hold the hall and let Toby go (he does
  not come back); call in Mama Tide's boats (needs Tideline rep 1+, and she bills you).
- **Lot Fourteen** (`a3_flats_2`, decision). *Choices:* walk the land-trust bid in, with Pier 9 as
  collateral; burn the site office and every lien record in it (heat +2); give Priya the ledger
  (if held).

**The last night**

- **The Wake** (`a3_wake`, decision; only if Jun is dead). *Choices:* take his coupe up Crestline
  Tower (straight to the "Ghost of Odile" ending); close the bay door and go and meet the storm.
- **Landfall** (`a3_landfall`, story, Nico). *Choices:* take the tug out and pull people off roofs
  (heat −1); use the blackout to settle accounts (heat +1, notoriety +1); take the seat on Nico's
  boat (leave town; warrants and patrons stay behind).
- **After the Water** (`finale`). The ending is selected from the final state; see
  `docs/ENDING_DESIGN.md`.

## 9. How the tracked state maps to the fiction

All numeric variables are small integers and are clamped to their range. A step of 1 is always a
story-sized event.

| Variable | Range (start) | In the fiction | Thresholds that matter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wealth` | 0–3 (0) | Cash and assets Cass controls. 0 living in the truck, 2 can fund a legal fight, 3 serious money. | 1+ to lease Jun's garage or cover Halloran's fee; 3 to make the Lantern Room an ending |
| `notoriety` | 0–3 (0) | How loudly the street says "Navarro." | 2+ to be worth hiring as Whitlock's fixer; 3 to hold the Tideline chair |
| `heat` | 0–4 (0) | Provable police attention on Cass personally. | 4 = arrested unless Cass leaves, has immunity from wearing the wire, or Sal takes the fall; 2 or less for the clean ending |
| `rep_tideline` | −1–2 (0) | Standing on the docks. −1 marked, 2 family. | 1+ to hold the chair, or to call in Tideline boats |
| `rep_crestline` | −1–2 (0) | Standing in the tower. −1 adversary, 2 on the org chart. | 2 to become the fixer |
| `rep_mpd` | −1–2 (0) | Standing with Halloran's side of the department. | 1+ for Halloran to keep Cass after Brandt is betrayed |
| `rep_flats` | −1–2 (1) | Standing in the Flats. Starts warm: Cass grew up there. | 1+ to inherit the Collective after Toby |
| `trust_jun` | 0–4 (2) | Whether Jun is treated as a partner. | 1 or less after the fire sends him to Hour Zero; 3+ and he leaves town with Cass |
| `trust_toby` | 0–3 (2) | Whether Toby still picks up. | 2+ to split the storm rescue |
| `trust_brandt` | 0–3 (0) | Whether Brandt will build a case around Cass. | 2+ at the end of Act 1 opens her branch; 1+ lets a Tideline defector through her door |
| `loyalty_sal` | 0–3 (1) | Whether Sal thinks Cass is Inés's equal. | 3 and he confesses in Cass's place |
| `allegiance` | 0–5 (0) | Who Cass currently works for: 0 nobody, 1 Tideline, 2 Crestline, 3 Brandt, 4 the Collective, 5 Halloran. | Routes the shared missions back to the right branch; several endings read it |

| Flag | Starts | Set when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `jun_alive`, `sal_alive`, `toby_alive` | true | Cleared in Hour Zero; Storm Warning; Emergency Powers or High Ground. |
| `owns_yard` | false | A patron, a court or a publication returns Pier 9 to Cass. |
| `owns_club` | false | Cass trades the ledger for Nico's stake in the Lantern Room. |
| `has_ledger` | false | Cass keeps the Odile Ledger; cleared when it is given to Priya or traded for Jun. |
| `informant` | false | Cass becomes Brandt's off-the-books informant; cleared on selling her out. |
| `tideline_made` | false | Cass takes the oath. Never cleared. |
| `crestline_partner` | false | Cass signs with Whitlock and surrenders the deed. |
| `land_trust` | false | The Mercy Street trust wins Lot Fourteen. |
| `whitlock_exposed` | false | The fraud is proven in public (wire, ledger or Vivian's affidavit). |
| `whitlock_dead` | false | Cass goes up the tower from Jun's wake. |
| `mama_tide_dead` | false | Cass takes the chair. |
| `halloran_arrested` | false | Cass wears the wire. |
| `priya_published` | false | The Odile Papers run. |
| `left_town` | false | Cass takes the seat on Nico's boat. |

Open-world play may nudge the numeric variables through `NarrativeState.adjust` (cash earned,
crimes witnessed, side-mission effects). Because ranges are small and thresholds sit at the ends,
such nudges should be rare and deliberate: one point of `heat` is the difference between a file
and a conviction.

## 10. The endings at a glance

Selection is deterministic: at the finale the endings are tested in descending priority and the
first whose requirements hold is the ending. Exact conditions, consequences, cinematics and an
example decision path for each are in `docs/ENDING_DESIGN.md`. In id order, Cass ends as:

01 *High Tide* (boss of the Tideline) · 02 *Navarro & Navarro* (legitimate owner, family intact) ·
03 *A Name That Isn't Yours* (witness protection) · 04 *Ironhook County* (prison) · 05 *Low Water*
(killed by the Tideline) · 06 *The Long Reach* (exile by boat with Jun) · 07 *Municipal Affairs*
(the Mayor's and Crestline's fixer) · 08 *The Flats Stand* (the yard becomes a community trust
depot) · 09 *Saltgrass* (burned-out drifter, alone) · 10 *Right Hand* (Mama Tide's heir apparent) ·
11 *Blue Wall* (Halloran's civilian contractor) · 12 *The Odile Papers* (vindicated, fourteen
months in county) · 13 *Ghost of Odile* (fugitive who killed Whitlock) · 14 *The Lantern Room* (the
city's new broker) · 15 *Ashes at Pier 9* (a two-bay tow shop with Jun) · 16 *The Inheritance*
(leads the Collective after Toby) · 17 *Glass Tower* (paid off and forgotten) · 18 *The Meridian
Accord* (holds the ledger and the peace) · 19 *The Old Man's Debt* (free because Sal confessed) ·
20 *Back on the Hook* (repo driver again; the fallback).

## 11. Style sheet

- **UNDERTOW: Port Meridian** in titles; "Port Meridian" in dialogue; never "PM."
- **the Rebuild** (capital R) for the money, agency and construction; **the Meridian Rebuild
  Authority** or "the Authority" for the agency alone.
- **Hurricane Odile** (past), **Hurricane Pilar** (present). People say "Odile" as a date: "before
  Odile," "since Odile."
- **the Tideline**; **Mama Tide** (to her face only once sworn; Sal alone says "Odalys").
- **Crestline** or "the tower"; **Phase Two** (Eastbank), **Phase Three** (Pier 9), **Phase Four**
  (the Flats frontage).
- **the Harbor Unit**; **the Benevolent Hall** or "the union hall."
- **the Flats Collective** or "the Collective"; **Mercy Street**; **Lot Fourteen**; **the Trust.**
- **Navarro Salvage**, **Pier 9**, "the yard"; the tug ***Inés***; the trawler ***Tres Marías***;
  the yacht ***Second Act***.
- **the Odile Ledger** (the green book); **the Odile Papers** (Priya's published series).
- Numbers: 206 liens; lien 141 is the Navarro lien; $412,000; Inés's signature dated four months
  after her death; 34 years of Navarro Salvage; 31st floor; slip forty; level four.
- American English in all player-facing text.
